# Measure and Plot Assignment 1

One PDF, organized in the three sections below, with the parts labeled as shown (1a, 1b, and so on).

In short: measure one thing ten times, work out the average and the spread, convert the average to other units, and make two plots.

**Purpose.** Measure the same quantity ten times under the same conditions and see how much the readings vary. That variation is measurement error, and it is what the mean and standard deviation describe.

**1. Measure.** Pick a quantity you can measure ten times with something you own. From your project if possible: the voltage your battery delivers, the time your mechanism takes to cycle, the mass of a part on a kitchen scale. Otherwise anything real: how long a kettle takes to boil, the length of a room by pacing it. Reset between readings and measure again. Note that something like reading the same ruler ten times without moving anything does NOT count: that gives ten copies of one number, not ten measurements.

1a. What you measured, with what instrument, and how. Include a photo of your setup.

1b. All ten readings, including the one that looks wrong. Do not clean up your numbers.

1c. Mean and standard deviation. Show your work (the formula with your numbers, or the spreadsheet formulas you used).

1d. Your result as mean ± standard deviation, with units. For example, 42.3 ± 0.6 s.

1e. Two or three sentences: where did the variation come from? The instrument, you, the environment, or the thing itself?

**2. Convert.**

2a. Convert your mean to the other unit system: SI if you measured in US customary units, US customary if you measured in SI (for example, 1.20 kg = 2.65 lb). Show the conversion, then one sentence on whether the number is sane.

**3. Plot.** Make two figures:

- **Figure 1:** your own ten readings from section 1 (details in 3a)
- **Figure 2:** the ball-drop data in the table below (details in 3b)

Any tool is fine (Excel, Google Sheets, Python, etc.), but say which one you used and how.

Both figures need:

- a caption under the figure, one or two sentences: what it shows, and why you picked that type of chart
- axis labels with units, for example "Time (s)"
- text large enough to read at the size you submit
- to still make sense printed in black and white, so don't rely on color alone to tell things apart
- no chart junk: no 3D effects, shadows, background pictures, or decoration that doesn't show data

3a. Figure 1: your own ten readings from section 1. Plot each reading against its trial number (1 to 10), so the reader can see how much the readings vary. Don't stretch or squash the vertical axis to make the spread look bigger or smaller than it is. If it is a project measurement, you can reuse this figure in your [presentation](project-presentation-and-demo.html) and [final report](project-specification.html).
